Why the dependency is in the world
  store.bclj declares no record; every type in its signatures is a fram.types
  record. Projected alone, its typed world fails types-refs-closed? and the
  source-to-typed pass rejects. The source world therefore carries both ASTs
  and names fram.store as its module.

Named gaps
  1  Bodies are not in the source world. The fact projection carries record
     and defn DECLARATIONS only, so no store body was ever presented to the
     lowering. Every one of the 71 store functions is a standing
     TODO-NATIVE-FUNCTION-BODY obligation; world-functions and world-abis are
     both 0 and typed-to-native is PENDING, not COMPLETE.
  2  obligation-world closed-layouts FAILs on the full world: shapes that need
     an explicit reference edge stay unclosed. The C17 projection restores the
     obligation by excluding them, which is why obligation-projection passes.
  3  The restricted C17 vocabulary admits 5 of 57 world types. Every shape
     store.bclj itself contributes -- (Vec T), closed (U T Nil), Text/Keyword
     atoms, map-entry vectors, (Atom T) references -- is outside it, so the
     emitted C is byte-identical to native-core/validation/slice-types-full's.
     The store-specific evidence in this slice is the sealed world, the typed
     signature surface, and the obligation ledger, not new C.
  4  main.c is hand-written: the materializer emits types only, so the runtime
     check is the layout claim under ASan/UBSan, never a store body.
